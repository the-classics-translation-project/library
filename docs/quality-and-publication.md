# Quality and publication checkpoints

The tools check **evidence and mechanical consistency**, not whether a book is good.
Do not combine all of the following into one percentage or “reviewed” checkbox.

Source identity/rights: know the acquired package, source edition and applicable
rights basis. Source integrity: preserve bytes and account for each normalized
block. Structural review: check whether the AI's hierarchy interpretation is right.
Coverage: account for all labels/body fields without pretending mere presence proves
semantic completeness. Fidelity and literary readability require substantive review.
Human-review claims require an actual human and a stated scope.

Before an intended reader release, resolve significant omissions and known problems,
record remaining qualifications and confirm source and new-edition rights/distribution
notes. Inspect HTML/EPUB on intended reading software. Run EPUBCheck when available;
package/XML checks alone are not EPUB conformance certification. Kindle rendering
and KDP metadata/disclosure requirements need their own actual tests/decisions.

Set an edition to `ready` only with the maintainer's approval. Freeze a new revision,
then explicitly approve its reader-catalogue entry. The validator refuses a reader
entry for a draft or a missing-field inventory. It cannot determine whether the
approval note is well founded; do not fabricate approvers or inspection results.

Review drafts can be distributed separately, clearly marked in catalogue and
metadata. A quiet contributor reading page need not display a verbose disclaimer
on every page; About/Help carries its status and provenance. Software fixtures are
excluded from normal site builds and are never presented as real classics.

`listed:false` hides a catalogue link, not its file. A public repository also
publishes committed snapshots and raw sources. Neither a separate contributor route
nor an unlisted revision URL makes text confidential.

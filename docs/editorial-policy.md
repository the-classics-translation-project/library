# Editorial policy

## Aim

Translate or modernize as though the original author had been born a contemporary
speaker of the target language and become a novelist. This is a working editorial
question, not a claim that we can reconstruct a uniquely correct answer.

## Scope

Choose public-domain works that the maintainer expects contemporary readers to
enjoy once the linguistic distance is reduced. Selection is a matter of taste and
project scope, not a universal judgment about a book's merit or accessibility.
Do not select a work that would require turning it into a substantially different
book to achieve that aim.

Preserve the story, characters, setting, narrative viewpoint, events and larger
narrative organization. Abridgement, new scenes, reordered chapters and structural
adaptation are outside the present scope. Retain the source's meaningful
Book/Volume/Chapter divisions and their order; translate existing book titles and
headings without inventing missing ones. Publishing the whole work as a single
EPUB does not erase its internal divisions. Contemporary language does not mean
moving the story into the present day.

Sentence boundaries, paragraphing and phrasing may change freely in service of
that aim. Do not preserve an awkward sentence merely to retain correspondence.
Do not mistake completeness for sentence-by-sentence literalism.

No universal house style, target sentence length, mandatory model or prescribed
translation procedure is established here. Record work-specific decisions only
when experience shows that they are useful. A whole new translation is a normal
possible revision, not a failure of the workflow.

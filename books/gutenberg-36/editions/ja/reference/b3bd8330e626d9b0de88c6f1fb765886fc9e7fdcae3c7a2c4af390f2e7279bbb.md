# The War of the Worlds — Japanese translation guide (`ja`)

**Working status:** All Japanese forms below are **provisional editorial suggestions, not approved translation decisions**. The supplied project notes establish the English work's *two-book, 17+10-chapter structure*, its front matter and its imported status; they establish **no published or human-approved Japanese translation or terminology policy**. Check existing Japanese editions and project preferences before freezing spelling, titles or voices. Source node IDs refer to the supplied structured work.

## Translation brief and register

Aim for **lucid, literary Japanese with an educated, retrospective first-person voice**, capable of moving from Edwardian-style essay and precise reportage into visceral terror. Prefer **「私」** as the narrator's consistent first-person self-reference in exposition. A restrained **だ／である** narrative register is a plausible starting point, but vary sentence rhythm, degree of formality and spoken pronouns according to situation. Do not mechanically reproduce every long English period as one sprawling Japanese sentence; preserve the **logic, suspense, repetitions and sudden perspective changes**. Avoid both excessive pseudo-Meiji archaism and casually contemporary SF jargon (`n0004`, `n0013`, `n0023`, `n0029`–`n0031`).

The narrator is *not* an omniscient hero. Distinguish **what he witnessed**, **what he was told**, **what he learned after the war**, **what he now believes**, and **what he admits remains open**. Japanese expressions such as **「らしい」「という」「後に知った」「おそらく」「断定はできない」** can preserve English hedging when warranted; avoid adding them to facts the source presents as direct observations. Conversely, do not turn speculations into plain declaratives. Keep the brother's separate itinerary in **third-person report**, rather than slipping into his first-person narration (`n0017`, `n0019`–`n0020`).

| H. G. Wells | **H・G・ウェルズ** | Keep the supplied byline (`n0001`). |
| Mars / Martian(s) | **火星／火星人** | 「火星人」 is the natural recurring designation; use **「火星の生物」「火星の侵略者」** selectively for the narrator's emphasis on their nonhuman anatomy, not as a gratuitous new species distinction. |
| Ogilvy / Henderson / Stent | **オギルヴィ／ヘンダーソン／ステント** | Scientists and journalist at the first cylinder; check published transliterations and consistency (`n0004`–`n0009`). |
| Mrs. Elphinstone / Miss Elphinstone | **エルフィンストン夫人／エルフィンストン嬢** | **Not the same woman**: the wife in white vs her capable younger sister-in-law. Japanese 「夫人」「嬢」 distinguishes forms of address, but use natural referential phrasing where appropriate (`n0019`–`n0020`). |
| the narrator's wife / my brother | **妻／弟** | Neither is given a personal name. Avoid invented names, and do not collapse the brother's direct reported adventures into the narrator's actions. |

| Woking / Horsell Common | **ウォキング／ホーセル・コモン** | *Common* means a shared-use open heath, **not** a park or “common sense.” On first mention explain it unobtrusively as 「共同利用の荒地」 or similar (`n0005`–`n0012`). |
| Maybury / Leatherhead | **メイベリー／レザーヘッド** | Home district vs the cousins' supposed place of safety (`n0012`–`n0013`, `n0030`). |

| Byfleet / Pyrford / Chobham / Ottershaw | **バイフリート／パーフォード／チョバム／オッターショー** | Local Surrey sites; provisional phonetic spelling, not an invitation to change them by chapter. |
| Halliford / Sheen / Mortlake | **ハリフォード／シーン／モートレイク** | Distinct waypoints of the narrator's route and imprisonment (`n0016`, `n0022`–`n0027`). |
| Putney Hill / Primrose Hill | **パトニー・ヒル／プリムローズ・ヒル** | **Do not confuse:** the artilleryman's lair is at the former; the final Martian redoubt at the latter (`n0028`–`n0029`). |
| the *Thunder Child* | **〈サンダー・チャイルド〉号** | **Ship's name**, not a child character. Prefer transliteration at first mention; the literal image 「雷の子」 could be explained only if wanted for a note (`n0020`). |

Other recurring landmarks (**the Spotted Dog**, **College Arms**, **Oriental College**, **Regent's Park**, **Hyde Park**, **Blackwater**, **Ostend**) should receive a consistent one-time style choice. E.g., **〈スポッテッド・ドッグ〉亭**, **〈カレッジ・アームズ〉**, **オリエンタル・カレッジ**, **リージェンツ・パーク**, **ハイド・パーク**, **ブラックウォーター河口**, **オーステンデ**. Avoid silently treating a tavern as a university college or converting *Oriental College* into a generic contemporary school (`n0012`–`n0015`, `n0020`). Geographic transliterations here are **suggestions for review**, not verified established Japanese exonyms.

## Technology, biology and recurrent imagery

The following is a **contextual concordance**, not an enforced one-to-one dictionary.

| Original expression | Provisional Japanese base | Usage and exceptions |
|---|---|---|
| **cylinder / projectile / shot** | **円筒／飛来体・砲弾** | The interplanetary vehicle is a hot, screw-topped *cylinder*, not initially an identified spacecraft. In artillery/astronomy narration *shot* may be **砲弾**; when its nature is not yet known use **物体／飛来体**. Retain the gradually revealed artificial nature (`n0004`–`n0007`). |
| **the Thing** (often capitalised) | **その物体／それ／あの得体の知れないもの** | Use local suspense and focalisation. A single rigid noun or premature **宇宙船** would flatten the discovery (`n0004`–`n0007`). |
| **fighting-machine** | **戦闘機械** | If silhouette/action matters, **三脚歩行機械** or **三脚の巨機**. Avoid bare **戦闘機**, which now primarily means an aircraft. The mechanism contains a living Martian (`n0013`–`n0015`, `n0023`). |
| **tripod** | **三脚式の歩行機械／三本脚の巨体** | Literal **三脚** alone can imply a static stand. Do not confuse machine legs with Martian anatomy (`n0013`, `n0023`). |
| **handling-machine** | **作業機械／多腕作業機械** | A many-limbed manipulating machine, later used to search the ruins. **操作機械** might misleadingly imply a control panel. Keep it distinct from the fighting-machine (`n0023`–`n0025`). |
| **digging-machine / flying-machine** | **掘削機械／飛行機械** | Separate specialised devices; the latter is still an **experiment** before the Martians die, not a deployed air fleet (`n0023`, `n0028`–`n0029`). |
| **Heat-Ray** | **熱線** | Capitalised named weapon: consistent **熱線**; **熱線発生装置** for its generator. The destructive beam is invisible, despite accompanying light and smoke. Do **not** introduce **レーザー** or assume a proven mirror mechanism (`n0008`–`n0009`, `n0031`). |
| **Black Smoke** | **黒い毒煙** (or recurring shorter **黒煙**) | The weapon is a **heavy lethal vapour**, deliberately released from canisters. In context 「毒性の黒い蒸気」「黒い煙」 can read more naturally than repeating a formula. **Not** ordinary smoke from burning buildings; distinguish the **黒い粉／粉末** remaining after deposition (`n0018`, `n0022`, `n0031`). |
| **red weed** | **赤い草／赤い異星植物** | No single Japanese plant category fits every appearance: when it creeps up walls **赤い蔓草**, on water **赤い植物の葉状体** may fit. First mention can retain a naming formula (e.g., **「赤い草」と呼ばれる植物**). Avoid automatically **藻** or **雑草** in contexts of giant fronds and flooding (`n0023`, `n0026`–`n0030`). |
| **red creeper** | **赤い蔓草／赤い這い植物** | **Keep the original term distinction visible** until the botanical relationship is established; the text itself describes this as a transitory growth and elsewhere uses it around trees (`n0023`, `n0027`–`n0028`). |
| **hood / cowl / head of the machine** | **頭部の覆い／覆い付きの操縦部** | It encloses a Martian operator; do not confuse the **metallic hood** with the Martian's actual fleshy head (`n0014`–`n0015`, `n0023`). |
| **carrier / cage / basket** | **収容籠／金属製の籠** | Where humans are seized, the rear carrier is a terrifying practical device, not the vehicle's main body (`n0022`, `n0024`). |
| **tentacles** | **触手** | Used for Martian bodily appendages and flexible mechanical arms; when clarity requires, say **機械の触手／金属の腕** as appropriate (`n0007`, `n0013`, `n0023`). |
| **hands** (Martian tentacle groups) | **「手」** | Professor Howes's name for the two groups of eight fine tentacles; keep quotation/technical context, not ordinary human hands (`n0023`). |
| **hoot / “Aloo!” / “Ulla, ulla…”** | **鳴き声・唸り声／「アロー！」／「ウーラ、ウーラ……」** | The sound spellings are *approximate Japanese phonetic proposals only*. Once chosen, keep their sonic identity. The last wail is eerie and repetitive, **not** a translated sentence; avoid inventing Martian linguistic meanings (`n0013`, `n0023`, `n0029`). |
| **torpedo ram / ironclad** | **水雷衝角艦／装甲艦** | Period naval terminology for *Thunder Child* and surrounding warships. Do not recast it as a contemporary destroyer or submarine (`n0020`). |
| **heliograph / hussars / sappers / Maxims** | **日光反射通信器／軽騎兵／工兵／マキシム機関銃** | Establish a compact period vocabulary and keep it intelligible in action sequences (`n0011`–`n0018`). |

The **Martian head-body, lungs, brain, sixteen tentacles, blood-injection feeding, absence of digestion, budding reproduction and lack of sleep** are presented in a retrospective quasi-scientific idiom; let the passages sound informed **without asserting that these are modern biological facts** (`n0023`). Repeated words such as **“injected,” “veins,” “recipient canal,” “bacteria,” “putrefactive,”** and **“natural selection”** call for sober precise Japanese (**注入、血管、受け入れる管、細菌、腐敗、自然選択**) rather than theatrical monster-film terms. The narrator's claim that Martians communicate by **thought without a physical medium** is **his conviction**, not a discovered universal law (`n0023`).

## Narrator, dialogue and embedded registers

- **Essayist / investigator:** articulate, exact, willing to qualify his knowledge. Keep contrasts such as microscopic life vs human empire, and the repeated shifts **“then I thought” / “we now know” / “afterwards I learned.”** For the opening's long comparison, the clauses may be broken into two or three Japanese sentences as long as the ironic symmetry survives (`n0004`, `n0010`, `n0023`, `n0031`).
- **Inner crisis:** plain, compressed cries and physical details often interrupt elaborate sentences. Avoid melodramatic embellishment. His dissociation, shame, guilt and trauma should not be rendered as a uniformly stoic or triumphalist narration (`n0010`, `n0013`–`n0014`, `n0025`, `n0028`–`n0031`).
- **Artilleryman:** slang, dropped initial *h*, *ain't*, blunt class judgments and self-satisfied rhetoric. Prefer **colloquial, clipped Japanese** (e.g. 「もう負けだ」「生き延びるんだ」), perhaps a touch of rough masculine phrasing, but **do not invent a Japanese regional dialect** or make him sound inarticulate: he can reason persuasively at length (`n0012`, `n0014`–`n0015`, `n0028`). The later irony depends on the same fluent voice coexisting with laziness.
- **Curate:** initially shocked, later increasingly compulsive, Biblical and prophetic: repetitions, cries of **woe**, references to Sodom and Gomorrah, the day of the Lord and repentance. Use **聖書調の韻律** where the source quotes scripture; verify quotations against any chosen Japanese Bible version before claiming canonical wording. The narrator's rebuke **“He is not an insurance agent”** is deliberate dry irreverence and should remain sharp (`n0016`, `n0025`).
- **Mrs. / Miss Elphinstone:** Mrs. Elphinstone is anxious and repeatedly invokes **George**; the younger woman's decisiveness is shown in action more than ornate speech. Do not automatically assign genteel, old-fashioned feminine endings such as **「〜ですわ」** to every line; distinguish their temperament and roles instead (`n0019`–`n0020`).
- **Crowds and minor speakers:** paper-sellers, soldiers and workers supply short dialect-marked utterances, newspaper headlines and rumor. Natural colloquial contrasts suffice; do not turn nineteenth-century London class and accent variation into a collection of unrelated Japanese dialect caricatures (`n0007`, `n0012`, `n0017`, `n0019`).
- **Newspapers and military reports:** headings are often sensational or misleading. Give them abrupt display formatting where appropriate, distinct from the narrator's own claims. Retain titles such as *The Times*, *Daily Telegraph*, *Daily Chronicle* and *Daily Mail* as period markers, adding Japanese glosses only where useful (`n0004`, `n0006`, `n0017`, `n0030`).

## Editorial watchpoints for Japanese continuity

1. **Retrospective science is not a corrected textbook.** Preserve period terms and limits of certainty, including the speculative parabolic Heat-Ray mirror, Martian thought exchange, possible Venus landing and alleged microbe-free Mars. In `n0029`, the narrator declares bacteria the destroyers; in `n0031` he explicitly says Carver's explanation is *not a proven conclusion*. A Japanese rendering must retain that change in epistemic status.
2. **A source-level spectral discrepancy needs a note, not a hidden correction:** **four blue spectral lines** for the unknown element in `n0018` versus **three green lines** in the black-powder analysis of `n0031`. Both are in the supplied text. Preserve both unless independent edition review establishes an authorized emendation.
3. **Do not prematurely resolve unresolved fates.** The wife ultimately lives despite a report of Leatherhead's destruction (`n0030`); the brother's life after embarkation and George Elphinstone's fate are not specified (`n0020`). The curate is struck with the **blunt** end of the chopper, then taken away, not depicted being stabbed (`n0025`). The third Martian by the naval battle is obscured (`n0020`).
4. **Historical dehumanising language should remain attributable.** The opening's **“inferior races”** is a racist nineteenth-century hierarchy introduced as part of the narrator's argument about human colonial extermination (`n0004`). A historically faithful **「劣等人種」** in context, potentially supported by an editorial note, preserves the disturbing wording more honestly than either endorsing it in neutral narration or disguising the racial hierarchy. Similarly preserve the artilleryman's harsh distinction between “fit” survivors and disposable “weaklings” as **his own speech**, not an editorial recommendation (`n0028`).
5. **Metaphors form a network.** Tiny creatures under the microscope, ants crushed by people, the dodo, a hunted rabbit, sheep, the colonised and the Martians' “eatable ants” all reverse humanity's presumed dominion (`n0004`, `n0010`, `n0018`, `n0027`–`n0028`). Preserve the exact animal chosen in each place: reusing one generic **「虫けら」** everywhere would flatten the motif.
6. **Distinguish belief, fear and irony.** The narrator's confidence in military defeat of the invaders, the curate's eschatology and the artilleryman's underground utopia are repeatedly exposed as partial or self-serving. Do not remove the narrator's bitter humour or self-implication for the sake of a unified heroic voice (`n0010`, `n0016`, `n0025`, `n0028`).
7. **Keep typography/measurement policy stable but reviewable.** Japanese dialogue can use **「　」**, interior citations **『　』**, and em dashes/ellipses as needed. Preserve meaningful headline breaks, the unfinished prophecy **“In about two hundred years …”** (`n0030`), capitalisation of named weapons through consistent terminology, and the epigraph's **Kepler — quoted in *The Anatomy of Melancholy*** attribution (`n0001`). Retain imperial distances, pounds sterling and period military designations initially; if editorial policy later adopts conversions or modernised orthography, apply it systematically rather than silently.

**Approval checkpoint:** Before chapter translation begins, confirm the published-title policy, canonical spelling of high-frequency proper names, whether **黒い毒煙** or **黒煙** is the preferred label, the strategy for **赤い草／赤い蔓草**, the two alien sound transcriptions, and Japanese narrative register. None has been approved by the supplied materials.

## Structure, narrative perspective and time

- **Two books, not two separate works.** Book One, **“The Coming of the Martians”** (`n0003`), has 17 chapters (`n0004`–`n0020`); Book Two, **“The Earth Under the Martians”** (`n0021`), has 10 (`n0022`–`n0031`). Chapter numbers restart in Book Two. The contents reproduce the chapter headings but are separate front matter. Preserve the supplied actual body headings and their punctuation as the structural source of truth; the Kepler quotation and its attribution belong before the contents (`n0001`–`n0002`).
- The main **first-person narrator is unnamed**. He lives with his unnamed wife at Maybury, near Woking, and writes speculative/philosophical essays. He recounts events **after** the invasion with hindsight, later research, reconstructed reports, direct address to the reader and pointed admissions of uncertainty. His narrative is not a continuous real-time diary. He sometimes steps back into an almost omniscient account, then qualifies what he could know (`n0004`, `n0011`, `n0018`, `n0030`–`n0031`).
- The narrative explicitly leaves his perspective to follow his **younger brother in London and on the eastward evacuation** (`n0017`, `n0019`–`n0020`). The switch is purposeful and marked by phrases such as “my brother”; these scenes must not be converted into experiences personally witnessed by the narrator. Book Two opens by returning to the narrator and the curate's confinement (`n0022`).
- The arrival happens in **June**, on a **Friday** (`n0005`–`n0011`, `n0020`, `n0024`). Observed Martian activity in 1894 precedes the attack; the narrator says the catastrophe was **six years ago** from his later vantage and situates the shock in the early twentieth century (`n0004`). These signals do not license inserting a precise calendar date. Ten discharges from Mars are reported, but the text does not give a complete individual landing narrative for all ten (`n0004`, `n0028`).

## Geographical continuity

**Arrival and Surrey fighting:** The first cylinder lands at the sandpits of **Horsell Common** near **Woking**; the narrator's house is at **Maybury**. Nearby **Ottershaw, Chobham, Byfleet, Pyrford, Addlestone, Weybridge, Shepperton, Chertsey** and the Rivers **Wey** and **Thames** matter to the advance, artillery lines and river escape (`n0005`–`n0018`). **Leatherhead** is initially imagined as safety for the narrator's wife but is later reported destroyed (`n0012`, `n0030`). The destroyed *Oriental College*, its mosque, the *Spotted Dog* inn, and the *College Arms* are named local landmarks, not generic settings (`n0012`–`n0015`).

**Narrator's Thames/London route:** After Weybridge he drifts downstream towards **Walton/Halliford** and joins the curate; they move through **Sunbury, Hampton Court, Twickenham, Richmond, Kew and Sheen**, where the **fifth cylinder** buries them beside its pit. After the confinement, he moves through **Mortlake, Roehampton and Putney**, reunites with the artilleryman near **Putney Hill**, then travels through **Fulham, South Kensington, Hyde Park, Regent's Park** and **Primrose Hill**, the site of the Martians' major London redoubt (`n0016`, `n0022`–`n0029`). He ultimately returns by railway via **Byfleet** to Woking (`n0030`). The narrative's shifting riversides and bridges are significant; do not flatten them to “around London.”

**Brother's separate route:** From London around **Regent's Park**, he attempts to flee via **Chalk Farm, Edgware and Barnet**, joins Mrs. and Miss Elphinstone, and heads through **Essex**, **Chelmsford** and **Tillingham** to the **Blackwater estuary/Essex coast**. Their crowded **paddle steamer** is bound for **Ostend**, witnessed during the naval engagement with *Thunder Child* (`n0017`, `n0019`–`n0020`). Distinguish **Putney Hill** from **Primrose Hill**, and the ordinary **common lands** from parks and streets.

## Event and causal anchors (not a complete synopsis)

1. **Before arrival:** Martian flashes observed telescopically; ten nearly daily launches go unrecognised as weapons. The first hot, screw-topped cylinder is misidentified as a meteorite; curious spectators expect humanoids (`n0004`–`n0007`).
2. **Friday–Saturday:** Ogilvy, Stent, Henderson and others approach with a white flag; the **Heat-Ray** annihilates them. News remains fragmentary. The military responds; Woking is devastated; the narrator sends his wife to Leatherhead and returns to find **giant walking fighting-machines** (`n0008`–`n0014`). The second cylinder arrives just after Friday midnight and the third during the narrator's Saturday-night storm (`n0011`, `n0013`).
3. **Sunday:** In the Weybridge–Shepperton battle, concealed guns kill **one Martian inside its fighting-machine**; the stricken machine crashes into the river. The other machines withdraw, carrying away its wreckage. The narrator meets the curate; his brother observes London's slow awakening (`n0015`–`n0017`).
4. **Sunday night–Monday:** The invaders fire canisters of **Black Smoke**, a heavy lethal vapour that follows low ground, and render organised defences ineffective. The fourth cylinder falls in Bushey Park; panic empties London. The narrator and curate become trapped when the fifth cylinder destroys most of a house near Sheen; the sixth lands at Wimbledon (`n0018`–`n0022`).
5. **Coastal escape:** The brother and Elphinstone women reach a steamer off Essex. The torpedo ram **_Thunder Child_** defeats **two Martian machines**, the second in its own fiery collision; the view of the ship and **third Martian** is then lost in steam and smoke. Their definitive fates are not observed in that scene (`n0020`). The artilleryman's later assertion that the Martians have lost only one reflects **his limited knowledge**, not a corrected casualty total (`n0028`).
6. **Confinement and reversal:** During about **fifteen days** in the buried kitchen/scullery, the narrator sees Martian industry and feeding, quarrels with the curate, strikes him, and narrowly escapes a probing mechanical tentacle. On emerging he finds an alien-looking landscape overrun with **red weed**; the weed begins to decline from a presumed terrestrial disease (`n0023`–`n0027`).
7. **Last phase and aftermath:** The artilleryman's underground civilisation is revealed to be more oratory than labour. The narrator finds stationary Martians in London and reaches the **Primrose Hill** redoubt: they are dead, attributed to Earth microorganisms. In later reflection the explanation is called **strongly probable, not conclusively proved**. He undergoes a period of mental disturbance, is told Leatherhead was destroyed, returns to his house and unexpectedly reunites with his wife. The epilogue leaves future invasion and a possible Venus landing open (`n0028`–`n0031`).

## Recurring systems and concrete distinctions

- **Martians themselves:** Not humanoids. Mature creatures have enormous head-bodies (about four feet across), large eyes, beak-like mouth, sixteen fine tentacles in two groups, lungs and central nervous structures; weak terrestrial mobility; no digestive system as humans understand it. They take **fresh blood from living prey**, including humans, directly into their own circulation. Reproduction by **budding**, absence of sex and sleep, and purported freedom from disease are presented in the narrator's scientific retrospective (`n0007`, `n0023`–`n0024`). Mars-based bipedal **food animals** found in cylinders are **not Martians** (`n0023`).
- **Different machines:** The towering three-legged **fighting-machine** carries the operator, Heat-Ray and sometimes a carrier for captives (`n0013`–`n0015`, `n0022`). The agile, many-armed **handling-machine** works on materials and can probe the house (`n0023`–`n0025`). Also distinguish a digging/excavating mechanism, material-processing equipment that makes aluminium from clay, and an experimental **flying-machine** at Primrose Hill (`n0023`–`n0024`, `n0028`–`n0029`). The narrator stresses their supple, nearly organic articulation and the **absence of wheels**, contrary to anachronistic mechanical imagery (`n0023`).
- **Heat-Ray:** An effectively invisible directed beam of destructive heat; solid materials burn/melt and struck water erupts as steam. A parabolic-mirror account is **a theory, not verified mechanism**. Green smoke, light and sparks seen around equipment are not themselves the same as the invisible beam (`n0008`–`n0009`, `n0031`).
- **Black Smoke:** Not ordinary fire smoke. A canister releases a dense poisonous vapour that accumulates in hollows, can leave upper storeys relatively safer, reacts with water and eventually settles as **black dust/powder**. Martians also disperse it by **steam jets**. Its chemical composition remains unsettled (`n0018`, `n0022`, `n0031`). Do not confuse the poisoning with heat damage or the dark soot of conventional fires.
- **Red weed / red creeper:** Extraterrestrial growth spreads spectacularly after the Martian landing, particularly through **flowing water**, clogs rivers and causes flooding, then decays, apparently under the influence of local bacteria (`n0023`, `n0026`–`n0030`). The text also names a **“red creeper,”** once described as shorter-lived; do not silently assume that every reference denotes the same plant or that all colour terms refer to one botanical structure (`n0023`, `n0027`–`n0028`).
- **Sounds:** The metallic machines produce hoots and **“Aloo! Aloo!”** (`n0013`); a late stationary Martian emits the unending, mournful **“Ulla, ulla…”** (`n0029`). The narrator's retrospective **telepathy hypothesis** contests others' assumption that hooting is Martian conversation; he claims the hoot precedes feeding, but it remains an inference (`n0023`). Do not interpret the sound strings as confirmed words or translate them into invented dialogue.
- **Nineteenth-century context:** Heliographs, telegraph wires, special newspaper editions, horse artillery, hussars, Maxim guns, steam railways, horse-drawn cabs, bicycles, early motor cars and naval ironclads coexist. The **torpedo ram _Thunder Child_** is a distinct historical-style *type of warship*, not a modern missile boat or aircraft (`n0011`–`n0020`).

## Voice, motifs and ethical tensions

The texture alternates **measured speculative-scientific exposition**, precise topographical reportage and quoted dispatches, vivid physical terror, socially distinctive dialogue and abrupt psychological confession. The narrator can describe whole populations with the cool eye of a naturalist and then break into personal panic. He repeatedly addresses a reader presumed to live after the catastrophe (`n0004`, `n0009`, `n0011`, `n0020`, `n0031`). Preserve the switches instead of smoothing everything into heroic action prose.

A central reversal is **humanity becoming the animal or colonised subject**: microscopy, insects, ants, rabbits, dogs, the extinct dodo and dispossession recur. The opening invokes the destruction inflicted by European colonists on Indigenous Tasmanians and uses a nineteenth-century hierarchy of “inferior races” (`n0004`); the narrator later learns pity for the creatures humanity dominates (`n0027`–`n0028`). These passages are uncomfortable historically situated language, not terminology to endorse or unobtrusively sanitise. Class prejudice, military bravado, unreliable sensational journalism, religious collapse/repentance, survivalist eugenic fantasies and the narrator's own violence invite critical distance (`n0012`, `n0016`–`n0019`, `n0024`–`n0028`).

The catastrophe does **not** end in a straightforward military victory. Powerful human weapons occasionally wound or destroy machines, but the decisive reversal is attributed to microbes. The narrator's delight at recovery coexists with dislocation and recurring traumatic visions; his future remains insecure even with his wife alive (`n0029`–`n0031`). The epilogue imagines both a **human cosmic future** and renewed subjection. Keep the final balance unresolved.

## Ambiguities and open research questions

- **Disease explanation:** The main narration presents bacterial defeat as definite (`n0029`), but the epilogue acknowledges **Carver's hypothesis remains unproved** (`n0031`). Mark the difference between assertive storytelling and formal epistemic status; do not silently eliminate it.
- **Spectral particulars:** The early Black Smoke account refers to an unknown element yielding **four lines in the blue** (`n0018`), while the epilogue's analysis of its **black powder residue** says **three lines in the green** (`n0031`). They may concern different material states/observations, or be a source inconsistency; preserve the text and flag for later edition checking rather than harmonising it.
- **Martian communication:** Sound vs telepathy remains narrator-inferred; **Venus colonisation** is Lessing's suggestion, not a confirmed sequel; the circumstances and aims behind the cessation of the ten shots remain unknown (`n0004`, `n0023`, `n0031`).
- **Unseen fates:** The wife's escape from Leatherhead, the brother's later movements after leaving by sea, George Elphinstone's fate, and the naval encounter's obscured third Martian are not supplied in full (`n0020`, `n0030`). Maintain narrative gaps.
- **Human culpability:** The curate's actual moment of death occurs outside view and the narrator evaluates his own blow defensively; the artilleryman's harsh programme is undercut by his behaviour, not confirmed as a feasible salvation (`n0025`, `n0028`). Avoid certainties of motive or culpability beyond the words on the page.
